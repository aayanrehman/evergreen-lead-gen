00Overview

What you're building

A repeatable loop: scrape every ad in your niche, score each by how long the advertiser kept paying for it, tag the angle and offer, learn what the survivors share, then generate your own creatives from those patterns, in your brand. It replaces the weekly "what should we post" scramble with a research step that runs itself.

Read this first

Longevity is a signal, not proof. An ad that has run 90 days is probably converting for someone, but you learn what works for you only by testing. The output is a batch of well-researched ads ready to test, not guaranteed winners. The Evergreen numbers in this guide are research findings; the campaign launches Oct 1.

01Scrape

Scrape the market

about 30 minabout $0.006 per ad

Run three passes so you catch both the loud advertisers and the quiet long-runners.

Pass 1Keywords

Named competitors plus niche phrases: "college essay help", "common app essay", "ivy league admissions".

Pass 2Competitor pages

Every live ad from the advertiser pages pass 1 surfaced. Use their Page IDs.

Pass 3Image-only

Static ads that are live now, sorted by impressions. Statics are what you can borrow structure from.

The Ad Library URL is your input. Sort by total impressions so survivors come first.

02Score

Score by how long ads survive

Advertisers switch off losing ads within a week or two. An ad still running after months is almost always earning its keep. Turn that into a score from public signals only.

python
import math

def win_probability(days_running, copies, is_live, advertiser_live_ads):
    z = (-3.0
         + 1.1 * math.log1p(days_running / 7)      # longer = better
         + 0.5 * math.log1p(copies - 1)            # same creative under many ad IDs = scaled
         + 0.6 * is_live                           # still running today
         + 0.3 * math.log1p(advertiser_live_ads))  # advertiser keeps investing
    return 1 / (1 + math.exp(-z))

"Copies" matters: advertisers scale a winner by duplicating it. These weights are hand-set, not trained on conversions. Treat 85% and up as proven enough to study.

03Tag

Tag every ad by angle

You want the ideas the survivors share, not just which ads. Start fast and free with keyword rules, then upgrade to questions for context.

python
ANGLES = {
  "Free guide / lead magnet": r"free guide|download|checklist|template",
  "Webinar / training":       r"webinar|training|masterclass|workshop|register",
  "Stat / proof":             r"\d+%|\d{2,3},?\d{3}\+?|\d+\+ (students|families|essays)",
  "Scarcity / deadline":      r"spots? left|limited|deadline|closing|last chance",
  "Parent-addressed":         r"\bparents?\b|your (child|son|daughter|teen)|families",
  "Free consult":             r"free consult|free call|book a|strategy session",
  "Question hook":            r"^[^.!\n]{0,90}\?",
  "Results / admits":         r"admitted|accepted|got in|acceptance letter",
}
prompt
Ad copy: {text}
Q1. Is this ad addressed to a parent (not the student)?
Q2. Does it offer something free before asking for a sale?
Q3. Does it state a concrete number or proof point?
Q4. Does it create real deadline pressure?
Answer each as a probability from 0 to 1.

Small, single-purpose questions beat one big "analyze this ad" prompt. The answers are comparable across thousands of ads, and a typed-probability model such as Jev makes that cheap and fast.

05Build

Let Opus pick and brief

Give Claude the scored, tagged table and your brand facts. Ask for structured output you can feed the next step.

prompt
You are the creative strategist for {brand}, {one-line description}.
Input: a table of {N} scored, tagged competitor ads (id, advertiser,
days_running, score, angles, copy).

1. Pick the 40 ads most worth learning from. Prefer high score, live, and angles
   the market under-uses. Return ids and one line on why each.
2. From them, write 28 hooks for parents of {audience}. Each under 12 words.
   No guarantees, no prices, no invented numbers, no em dashes.
3. Return 26 creative briefs as JSON: {layout, headline, subline, cta, angle,
   image_scene, aspect, destination}. Borrow structure only. Never copy wording
   or another brand's assets.
Facts you may use: {approved facts only}.
Guardrails

Give the model a short list of approved facts (credentials, real numbers, real reviews) and forbid everything else. Models will happily invent testimonials and statistics.

06Statics

Generate the statics

Use the image model for the scene and keep text exact. Two reliable patterns: generate the background and set the text in HTML so spelling is perfect, or let the model render text but quote the exact strings and proofread every glyph.

prompt
Editorial photograph, natural window light. Palette: deep evergreen #1F4A3C,
warm paper white, soft sage, brass accent #B8935A. {scene}. Calm, premium.
Leave the left 55% as clean negative space for a headline.
No text, no logos, no people's faces toward camera. Aspect ratio {4:5}.
DoKeep brand tokens fixed

Same colors and two fonts on every ad, so the batch reads as one company.

DoMake two ratios

4:5 for Feed, 9:16 for Reels and Stories. Square ads look small in Reels.

NeverFake proof

No real people's faces or lookalikes, no fake reviews or acceptance letters, no university logos.

07Reels

Render the reels

HyperFrames turns HTML into video, so a reel is a web page with a timeline: your fonts, your colors, text you can edit and re-render in seconds. Brief it like a designer: the message, the length (30 to 35 seconds), the words on screen and the brand tokens. If you use an AI voice, keep it clearly synthetic.

09Results

Read results and keep going

  1. 1Days 1 to 3Change nothingThe learning phase. Answer comments.
  2. 2Day 4First readJudge by cost per consultation request, then link CTR (aim for 0.9% or better) and landing-page conversion (3 to 8% is healthy for a short form).
  3. 3Day 8Cut and addPause the weakest third of ads. Add new ones from your next scrape.
  4. 4Day 15Scale carefullyRaise a winner by at most 20% every three days. Don't duplicate ad sets.

Track downstream in a sheet: request, call booked, call held, client, per utm_content. Re-run the scrape every two to four weeks. Your best new ads come from what has started surviving.

10Guardrails

Rules, limits, mistakes

Borrow structure, not content

Layout, hierarchy and angle are fair game. Copying wording, images or another brand's identity is not.

No guarantees, no invented proof

Use only real reviews, real numbers and real credentials. Have the model work from an approved fact list.

Longevity is a heuristic

A long-running ad can be profitable for one advertiser and wrong for you. Test small before you scale.

Judge by clients, not clicks

Clicks and downloads are easy to buy. Track requests that become calls that become clients.

Disclosures and data

Photorealistic AI images may be labelled "AI info" by Meta. Don't put a real person's likeness in generated ads. Scrape only public Ad Library data and follow the terms of the actor you use.
